I WAS A LITTLE PIRATE

ok so as a kid i pirated practically everything. music. movies. games. software. EVERYTHING.

i downloaded a pirated copy of Microsoft Word just so i could do my schoolwork. (yes really. homework was the reason.)

i played Minecraft through knockoff launchers and downloaded mods from places i should NOT have been downloading mods from.

my dad wasn't really interested in spending money on software or games and as a kid i obviously didn't have a credit card or any way to buy this stuff myself.

so i spent an unreasonable amount of time troubleshooting malware, viruses, broken installs, and whatever else i accidentally downloaded that day.

the computer my dad bought me became my playground and my personal DISASTER ZONE.

BUT I WAS MISSING OUT

ok this part is a little less funny. i want to be honest about it.

i didn't get to experience some of the things my friends did.

i couldn't play legit Minecraft multiplayer with them. i couldn't join certain online games.

i remember being stuck playing campaign mode in Call of Duty: Black Ops by myself while everyone else was playing multiplayer together.

as a kid, that genuinely hurt.

i wasn't just missing out on software. i was missing out on experiences and memories with other people.

>> FAST FORWARD >>

that kid eventually became a software developer. (crazy, i know)

the same curiosity that made me troubleshoot broken games and sketchy installers turned into an interest in engineering.

i started making websites, messing around with Linux, building apps, poking at infrastructure, and eventually making my own open-source projects.

FULL DISCLAIMER: i pay for my software now. i am a functioning adult. mostly.

but i never forgot what it felt like to want access to something and not have the means to get it.

that's part of why i love open source. i love that people build things, share them, let others learn from them, change them, and make whole new things on top of them.

i want to be part of that.
